# TesorerIA contracts

`Fund` holds a group's shared money in wARS (and USDT until the agent converts it) on Celo. The agent can only:
- convert USDT to wARS within 2% of the pool's 30-minute TWAP;
- reimburse the expense the group agreed on, to a member, up to a weekly cap.

Everything else (other payments, member changes, the weekly cap, quorum, replacing or firing the agent, closing the fund) needs N-of-M member votes, with N always below M so one lost passkey can't lock the fund; the total cap is fixed when the fund is created. The agent can propose these actions but never votes. Once the fund is closed, anyone can call `distributeRemainder` to split, by the same rule, whatever reaches it later. `FundFactory` deploys one `Fund` per group.

Version 2 (`FundV2` and `FundFactoryV2`, the factory deployed at `0x1337a61CF7fDeF412E04894FE705d4341878d4d3`) sits next to them: see [Version 2](#version-2), which starts with its known risks.

## Security review

Internal review before the first mainnet deploy, not an external audit: Slither 0.11.6 and the Pashov checklist over `src/`. It was first done on 2026-10-08 and redone after the pre-deploy changes: N below the member count, `distributeRemainder`, and a swap callback that pays at most the converted amount, once. No finding needed a code change. Line numbers are in `src/Fund.sol` unless noted.

### Slither

`slither . --filter-paths "lib/|test/" --exclude-informational` reports 13 results: 2 High, 8 Medium and 3 Low, all justified below.

- **High, `reentrancy-balance` in `convert` (2 results, one per swap direction; `:246`, `:248-254`, `:257`).** False positive. Reading the wARS balance before and after `pool.swap` is how the fund measures what actually arrived. Nothing can take wARS out of the fund during the swap: `convert` holds the `nonReentrant` lock, as does every other function that moves tokens except the swap callback, and the callback pays USDT only to the pool, only while `convert` runs, at most `usdtAmount` and only once (`:263-269`). An outside transfer during the swap could only add wARS. The pool address is immutable. The real wARS and USDT have no transfer hooks today, but both are upgradeable (see Centralization).
- **Medium, `incorrect-equality` (5 results).** False positive. `total == 0` in `_split` (`:465-466`) and `totalContributed == 0` in `_shareOf` (`:421`) compare internal accounting that a token transfer cannot change, and guard a division by zero. `usdtAmount == 0` in `quoteUsdtInWars` (`:185`) is an early return; a USDT donation only turns a zero into the correct quote of the donated amount. `bw == 0 && bu == 0` in `distributeRemainder` (`:451`) only rejects a call with nothing to split; a donation just gives the call something to split.
- **Medium, `unused-return` (3 results).** Intended. `_averageTick` (`:198`) only needs the tick cumulatives from `pool.observe`. `convert` ignores the amounts returned by `pool.swap` (`:248-254`, 2 results): the wARS received is measured by balance difference and checked against `minWarsOut` (`:256-257`), and the callback checks the USDT side itself: it pays only the USDT delta, only if it is positive and at most `usdtAmount` (`:266-267`).
- **Low (3 results).** `reentrancy-benign`: `_swapUsdtLimit = 0` is written after the swap (`:255`) on purpose, since the limit has to cover it; it lives in transient storage, so it can't outlive the transaction either. `timestamp`: proposal expiry (`:361`) uses `block.timestamp` on purpose (proposals last 7 days, so seconds of drift don't matter); the flagged `id >= _proposals.length` checks (`:347`, `:358`) don't involve time.

The 20 forge-lint warnings in `src/` were also reviewed and left as they are. Every `unsafe-typecast` is bounded: `uint128` is checked at `:186`, the TWAP tick fits `int24`, `int256(usdtAmount)` is capped by the USDT balance, the callback casts the USDT delta only after checking that it is positive and at most `usdtAmount`, the `uint8` `votesRequired` is below 10, and the `uint64` is a timestamp. The modifiers before `nonReentrant` only read storage. The `reentrancy-events` sit in `nonReentrant` functions or, in `FundFactory.sol:33`, after a constructor that only makes view calls. The rest repeat Slither's `unused-return` and `timestamp`, plus a revert inside the constructor's validation loop.

### Pashov checklist

1. **Reentrancy: PASS.** Every function that moves tokens is `nonReentrant` (OpenZeppelin `ReentrancyGuardTransient`): `contributeWars`, `contributeUsdt`, `convert`, `reimburseAgreedExpense`, `execute` and `distributeRemainder` (`:205`, `:210`, `:237`, `:279`, `:326`, `:448`), except the swap callback, which only runs inside `convert`: instead of the lock, it requires `msg.sender == pool` and a pending limit that `convert` sets and the first payment clears (`:264-268`). Tests have a third party call it in the middle of a conversion, and the pool ask for more than `usdtAmount` or collect twice. State changes come before transfers in reimbursements (`:286`), execution (`:329`), member removal (`:380-383`) and closing (`:440`). Contributions pull the tokens before crediting, to credit what actually arrived; the lock covers them.
2. **Overflow: PASS.** Solidity 0.8.37 checked arithmetic. `src/` has no `unchecked` blocks; the only ones on its paths are in Uniswap's `TickMath.getSqrtRatioAtTick` and `FullMath.mulDiv`, reached through `OracleLibrary.getQuoteAtTick`. Narrowing casts are bounded (see forge-lint above).
3. **Access control: PASS.** `onlyMember` (`:130`) guards contributions and votes, `onlyAgent` (`:135`) guards conversion and reimbursement, `propose` takes members or the agent (`:303`), and `whenOpen` (`:140`) guards every state change except the swap callback, which only runs inside `convert`, and `distributeRemainder`, which only runs once the fund is closed (`:449`). Anyone can call `execute`, but it needs N votes (`:328`); anyone can call `distributeRemainder`, but it only splits by the close's rule. The constructor checks 3 to 10 distinct non-zero members, an agent who is not one of them, 2 ≤ N < members, a non-zero total cap and the pool's tokens (`:147-160`). The factory has no privileged functions.
4. **Front-running and MEV: PASS.** `convert` rejects a `minWarsOut` below 98% of the 30-minute TWAP quote (`:243`) and reverts if less than `minWarsOut` arrives (`:257`). The swap has no price limit (`:252`), so a sandwich can take at most the gap between the market price and `minWarsOut`; the agent should set `minWarsOut` near the current quote, not at the floor. USDT contributions are credited at the TWAP, not the spot price. Reimbursements, payments and the closing split don't depend on prices.
5. **Oracle manipulation: PASS.** The only price is the pool's 30-minute TWAP from `observe` (`:195-202`), rounded toward negative infinity like Uniswap's `OracleLibrary.consult`. It sets USDT credits, the total cap check and the conversion floor. A price moved within one block barely moves it, but the pool is thin, so holding a moved price for longer may be cheap. What bounds the damage is the fund's total cap (`balanceCap`, fixed at creation: the app will propose 450,000 wARS, about US$300, but the contract accepts any non-zero cap), the vote needed to take any gain out of the fund (`RemoveMember` or `Close`), and the agent's own comparison with Chainlink before converting, which lives off-chain, in the agent. The oracle can also be blocked for a while: the real pool keeps 1,200 observations, at most one per block, so touching it in every ~1 s block leaves only about 20 minutes of history, and then `observe` reverts (`OLD`), as it does for a pool with less than 30 minutes of history. Meanwhile `contributeUsdt`, `convert` and `contributeWars` (when the fund holds USDT, through `balanceInWars`) revert too, but votes, payments, reimbursements and the exits (`RemoveMember`, `Close`, `distributeRemainder`) don't use the oracle: a denial of service, not a loss. Anyone can enlarge the pool's buffer with `increaseObservationCardinalityNext`.
6. **Unchecked external calls: PASS.** Every token movement uses `SafeERC20`: `safeTransferFrom` (`:219`) and `safeTransfer` (`:269`, `:293`, `:384-385`, `:467-468`). Incoming amounts are measured by balance difference (`:218-220`, `:246`, `:256`); a test token that burns 1% of every transfer is credited only what arrived. The ignored returns of `pool.swap` and `pool.observe` are covered under Slither.
7. **Centralization: PASS.** `Fund` and `FundFactory` have no owner, admin, pause or upgrade path. The tokens do: wARS and Celo's USDT are upgradeable EIP-1967 proxies, so their issuers can change them, for example to charge transfer fees, add transfer hooks, pause transfers or blocklist addresses (see 9). Contributions credit only what arrives (`:218-220`), but a payout could deliver less than the fund sends. The agent can only `convert` (the wARS stay in the fund), `reimburseAgreedExpense` (to a member, within `weeklyCap` per week, `:281-286`) and `propose`. It never votes: only a member's proposal carries the proposer's vote (`:318`), and `vote` is `onlyMember` (`:321`). It can never be a member (`:157`, `:372`, `:388`, `:411`), and members replace or fire it with a `SetAgent` vote. A stolen agent key can cost at most `weeklyCap` a week, paid to members, plus up to 2% per conversion against the TWAP; an attacker who also holds the pool's price for 30 minutes (see 5) moves that floor too, and can lose the group up to all the USDT the fund holds at that moment, which `balanceCap` limits. N always stays below the number of members (`:153`, `:379`, `:407`), so one lost passkey can't block the vote that replaces that member; a group of 3 (N = 2) can replace a member but not remove one.
8. **Events: PASS.** Every state change emits an event: `Contribution`, `Conversion`, `Reimbursement`, `ProposalCreated`, `VoteCast` and `ProposalExecuted`; each executed action adds its own (`Payment`, `MemberAdded`, `MemberRemoved`, `MemberReplaced`, `WeeklyCapChanged`, `VotesRequiredChanged`, `AgentChanged`, `FundClosed`); `distributeRemainder` emits `RemainderDistributed`; and the factory emits `FundCreated`. `FundClosed(warsBalance, usdtBalance)` and `RemainderDistributed(warsBalance, usdtBalance)` emit the balances read before the split, including the rounding dust that stays behind. Tests pin the exact data of `Contribution`, `Conversion`, `Reimbursement`, `ProposalCreated`, `VoteCast`, `Payment`, `ProposalExecuted` and `RemainderDistributed`.
9. **Tokens with a blocklist: FAIL (known risk, governance workaround).** wARS has a `COMPLIANCE_ROLE` (off today) and Celo's USDT has a blocklist (`isBlocked`). Payouts are pushed, so if a member who is owed a share gets blocklisted, closing (`:467-468`) and removing that member (`:384-385`) revert for everyone, as do payments to them. Workaround: vote `ReplaceMember` to another address of the same member (it moves no tokens), then close. After closing, members can't be replaced any more, so a member blocklisted later blocks `distributeRemainder` for both tokens whenever they are owed a share of the blocked token, until the blocklist is lifted. If the fund itself were blocklisted, its balance of that token would be frozen and the contract can't prevent it.

## Version 2

`FundV2` and `FundFactoryV2` let each group pick its currency: any wFIAT, with or without a dollar entry, plus a fee for the gas. They sit next to `Fund` and `FundFactory`, which stay as deployed: the real group's funds are on them.

`FundFactoryV2` is deployed on Celo mainnet at `0x1337a61CF7fDeF412E04894FE705d4341878d4d3` (transaction `0x4be952e427f472305e40bbe3f1c2563d03287ed692d1548707da59fd4bc129be`, block `83102288`), by the agent's wallet, with the ERC-8021 attribution suffix after the constructor arguments. Its constructor took Uniswap's factory `0xAfE208a311B21f13EF87E33A90049fC17A7acDEc`, which it checks every pool against, and the fee recipient `0x5Dc9C76C6999C6C604b3D91C040D17b125ca4581`, TesorerIA's own account: both are immutable, and there is no owner. It was deployed with `pnpm deploy:factory-v2 --send` from `packages/agent`, which first checks the fee recipient and each dollar pool, and refuses a build whose size isn't the reviewed one. Its source is verified on Sourcify (exact match of the deployed code), and [Blockscout](https://celo.blockscout.com/address/0x1337a61CF7fDeF412E04894FE705d4341878d4d3?tab=contract) shows it as verified. Every `FundV2` it creates runs the same code.

### Known risks

What can still go wrong with a version 2 fund, before the rest. Each one is argued in the item of [the checklist](#pashov-checklist-1) it names.

- **A stolen agent key, while the pool's price is held for 30 minutes.** On top of `weeklyCap` a week and up to 2% per conversion, an attacker who also holds the pool's price for 30 minutes can lose the group all the dollars pending at that moment. `balanceCap`, fixed when the fund is created, is the ceiling on that loss (the app proposes 450,000 wARS, about US$300). Items 5 and 7.
- **A blocklisted member or fund blocks the exits.** Celo's USDT has a blocklist, and wARS has a compliance role (off today). Payouts are pushed, so if a member who is owed something gets blocklisted, closing and removing that member revert for everyone until the group votes `ReplaceMember` to another address of theirs, and then the close goes through. After the close members can't be replaced any more, so a member blocklisted later blocks `distributeRemainder` whenever they are owed anything, until the blocklist is lifted. If the fund itself were blocklisted, its balance of that token would freeze and, while it holds any, so would the close. There is no admin to rescue it, by design. Item 9, the checklist's one FAIL. Version 1 has the same risk (item 9 of [its checklist](#pashov-checklist)).
- **The tokens have to be standard.** `_split` and the close assume that the currency and the dollar are plain ERC-20s: no rebasing down, no fee charged to the sender, no issuer taking tokens out of the fund. They are today, but both are upgradeable: if the dollar stopped being, the fund couldn't close; if the currency did, `availableCurrency()` could underflow, once the currency balance falls below `feeOwed`, and almost nothing would work. Item 10.
- **Dust after the close.** The split rounds down and leaves a few wei, and anyone can call `distributeRemainder` again at their own gas cost, possibly moving nothing: those calls are noise. Item 12.
- **The pool may not keep 30 minutes of history.** The real pool stores 1,200 observations, at most one per block, so a price that changes in every block of about a second leaves only about 20 minutes, and `observe` reverts (`OLD`) until it has more. The calls that read the TWAP stop meanwhile (item 5 lists them), but votes, payments and exits don't: a denial of service, not a loss. The deploy script checked that each dollar pool serves the 30 minutes and keeps at least 6 hours of history, which doesn't rule this out. Version 1 has the same risk (item 5 of [its checklist](#pashov-checklist)). Anyone can enlarge a pool's buffer with `increaseObservationCardinalityNext`; whether to do it for TesorerIA's pool is still open.

### What changes from v1

- **Any currency.** `createFund` takes the fund's `currency` and, optionally, a `dollar` and the Uniswap v3 `pool` of the two. The factory checks that the pool is the one Uniswap's factory has for that pair at its fee tier (`FundFactoryV2.sol:47`) and that it already serves a 30-minute TWAP (`FundFactoryV2.sol:50-55`). A fund without a dollar never reads a pool: `contributeDollar` and `convert` revert with `NoDollar`, and dollars sent to it directly are ignored.
- **Pending dollars.** `contributeDollar` keeps each member's dollars in `pendingDollar` and credits nothing until the agent converts them. `convert(members, dollarAmounts, minCurrencyOut)` swaps several members' dollars in one swap, with v1's 2% TWAP floor, and credits each member `received * amount / total`, rounded down, net of the fee. A swap that doesn't take all the dollars reverts.
- **The fee.** Each fund has a `feeBps`, fixed at creation and at most `MAX_FEE_BPS` (1%). It is set aside in the currency from every contribution and every converted share, and `contributed` counts the net amount. The fee accrues in `feeOwed`, and anyone can call `payFee()` to send it to the factory's `feeRecipient`, with the fund open or closed. What the fund can pay or split is `availableCurrency()`: the currency balance minus `feeOwed`.
- **The cap.** `balanceCap` limits the available currency plus the pending dollars at the 30-minute TWAP, and both `contribute` and `contributeDollar` check it. Dollars sent to the fund directly belong to nobody: they never count toward the cap and never block a contribution, and the close splits them with the rest. With nothing pending, `contribute` never reads a pool. `balanceInCurrency()` is a separate view of the fund's full value: the available currency plus every dollar it holds, pending or not, at the TWAP.
- **`GiveBack`** (action 8; 0 to 7 are as in v1) pays a member back part of what they contributed, by vote, and lowers their `contributed` by the same amount.
- **Exits.** A removed member gets their share of the available currency and all their own pending dollars; a replacement takes over both. The close and `distributeRemainder` pay each member's pending dollars first, then split the available currency and any dollars no one has pending by `contributed`. `feeOwed` stays for `payFee`.
- **Limits.** `SetAgent` rejects the zero address. `weeklyCap` can't exceed `balanceCap`, at creation or by vote. Each member and the agent can make at most `MAX_PROPOSALS_PER_WEEK` (10) proposals a week, counted from zero again each week, with notes of at most `MAX_NOTE_BYTES` (560).
- **Events** keep v1's types where they can, so the same decoders read them. `Conversion` (one per member, with `credited`) and `FundCreated` (with `currency` and `dollar`) change, and `ContributionGivenBack` and `FeePaid` are new.

The tests are in `test/v2/` (109 unit and fuzz tests and 6 invariants) and `test/fork/FundV2Fork.t.sol` (5 tests on a fork of Celo mainnet, with the real wARS pool and a wMXN fund without a dollar). The commands above run them along with v1's.

### Security review of version 2

Internal review on 2026-11-17, before the deploy of v2, not an external audit: Slither 0.11.6, forge-lint and the Pashov checklist over `FundV2` and `FundFactoryV2`. The final pass did change code, in two places: the balance cap now counts only the dollars members have pending, since dollars sent straight to a fund belong to nobody and used to count, so a single transfer could have blocked every contribution; and the constructor now rejects the fund's own address as the agent or as a member, as `SetAgent` and the member changes already did. Nothing else needed a change. Line numbers are in `src/FundV2.sol` unless noted.

#### Slither

With v2 in `src/`, the same command reports 32 results: v1's 13, unchanged and justified above, and 19 in v2 (4 High, 12 Medium and 3 Low), all justified below.

- **High, `reentrancy-balance` in `convert` (4 results: the currency and the dollar balance, for each swap direction; `:333-334`, `:336-342`, `:347-349`).** False positive, as in v1. Reading both balances around `pool.swap` is how the fund measures what the swap delivered, and checks that it took exactly the dollars taken off the members' pending (`:347`). Each member's share is rounded down first, so the shares never add up to more than what arrived, and the fee is then taken from that share, as the spec says, so `credited + fee == share`. The precision lost is under 1 wei of fee per member, in the member's favor.

- **Medium, `unused-return` (4 results).** Intended. `_averageTick` (`:266`) only needs the tick cumulatives from `pool.observe`. `convert` ignores the amounts returned by `pool.swap` (`:336-342`, 2 results): it measures both sides by balance difference (`:347-349`), and the callback checks the dollar delta itself (`:406`). The factory's `_checkPool` (`FundFactoryV2.sol:52-55`) only needs `observe` not to revert, which is what makes the TWAP usable from the first day.
- **Low (3 results).** `reentrancy-benign` in `convert`: `_swapDollarLimit = 0` is written after the swap (`:343`) on purpose, since the limit has to cover it, and it lives in transient storage; the credits (`:393-397`) come after the swap because they depend on what it delivered, and the lock keeps every other function that moves tokens out until they are written. `timestamp`: proposal expiry (`:507`) uses `block.timestamp` on purpose; the flagged `id >= _proposals.length` checks (`:493`, `:504`) don't involve time.

The 8 `unsafe-typecast` are bounded as in v1: `uint128` is checked at `:240`, the TWAP tick fits `int24`, `int256(total)` is capped by the pending dollars, the callback casts the dollar delta only after checking that it is positive and at most `total`, the `uint8` vote count is below 10, and the `uint64` is a timestamp. The 5 modifiers before `nonReentrant` only read storage. The 4 `reentrancy-events` sit in `nonReentrant` functions or, in `FundFactoryV2.sol:38`, after a constructor and pool checks that only make view calls. The 4 `require-revert-in-loop` validate input (`:201`, `:364-370`), which must revert the whole call. The 3 `missing-events-arithmetic` (`:374`, `:396-397`) are totals without an event of their own, which the events of every change let a reader rebuild (see Events below). The `incorrect-strict-equality` is the exact-debit check (`:347`), which has to be strict. The rest repeat Slither's `divide-before-multiply`, `uninitialized-local`, `unused-return` and `timestamp`.

#### Pashov checklist

1. **Reentrancy: PASS.** Every function that moves tokens is `nonReentrant`: `contribute`, `contributeDollar`, `convert`, `reimburseAgreedExpense`, `execute`, `distributeRemainder` and `payFee` (`:276`, `:290`, `:324`, `:419`, `:472`, `:621`, `:660`), except the swap callback, which only runs inside `convert`: instead of the lock, it requires `msg.sender == pool` and a pending limit that `convert` sets and the first payment clears (`:403-407`). Tests have a third party call it in the middle of a conversion, and the pool ask for more than `total` or collect twice. State changes come before transfers in reimbursements (`:426`), execution (`:475`), `GiveBack` (`:547-548`), member removal (`:578-586`), the split (`:613`, `:644`, `:652`) and `payFee` (`:663`). Contributions pull the tokens before crediting, and `convert` credits after the swap, both under the lock.
2. **Overflow: PASS.** Solidity 0.8.37 checked arithmetic. `src/FundV2.sol` and `src/FundFactoryV2.sol` have no `unchecked` blocks; the only ones on their paths are Uniswap's, as in v1. Narrowing casts are bounded (see forge-lint above). The subtractions that could underflow have a check before them (`:370`, `:433`, `:546`) or rely on the backing of `totalPendingDollar` and `feeOwed` (see 10).
3. **Access control: PASS.** `onlyMember` (`:164`) guards contributions and votes, `onlyAgent` (`:169`) guards conversion and reimbursement, `propose` takes members or the agent (`:446-447`), and `whenOpen` (`:174`) guards every state change except the swap callback, `distributeRemainder` (only once the fund is closed, `:622`) and `payFee`, which anyone can call at any time but which only sends `feeOwed` to the immutable `feeRecipient` (`:661-664`). Anyone can call `execute`, but it needs N votes (`:474`). The constructor checks 3 to 10 distinct non-zero members, none of them the agent or the fund itself, 2 ≤ N < members, an agent that is neither zero nor the fund itself, total cap and `feeRecipient`, `weeklyCap ≤ balanceCap`, `feeBps` at most 1%, a non-zero currency other than the dollar, the dollar and the pool both set or both zero, and a pool of exactly those two tokens (`:183-204`). The factory has no privileged functions. Anyone can create a fund with any tokens: the factory checks the pool against Uniswap's factory, but not which currency or dollar it is, so an app must check them against the tokens it knows before showing a fund. The constructor and the factory only make view calls to the pool, so a fake pool can only make `createFund` revert. That holds for the funds the factory creates. A `FundV2` deployed directly skips the factory's `getPool` and `observe` checks, so a fake pool there could set the TWAP and receive the swap callback: the app must serve only the funds listed in `FundFactoryV2.funds`.
4. **Front-running and MEV: PASS.** `convert` rejects a `minCurrencyOut` below 98% of the 30-minute TWAP quote of the whole `total` (`:329-330`) and reverts if less than `minCurrencyOut` arrives (`:349`). The swap has no price limit (`:340`), so a sandwich can take at most the gap between the market price and `minCurrencyOut`; the agent should set `minCurrencyOut` near the current quote, not at the floor. Every member in one conversion gets the same price per dollar (`:388`). Dollars are credited at what the swap delivers, not at the TWAP as in v1, so a dollar contribution can't be timed against the oracle. A TWAP quote of 0 would let `minCurrencyOut = 0` through, as in v1. A quote of 0 means the TWAP values the whole conversion at under 1 wei of the currency, so with an honest TWAP a floor of 0 risks less than that; a TWAP moved that far is the risk in 5. Payments, reimbursements, the fee and the split don't depend on prices.
5. **Oracle manipulation: PASS.** The only price is the pool's 30-minute TWAP (`:263-270`), computed as in v1. In v2 it only sets the cap check (`:257-260`, on the available currency and the pending dollars) and the conversion floor (`:329-330`): members are credited what the swap delivers (`:348`, `:388`), so a moved TWAP can't inflate anyone's share. It can only loosen the floor or misjudge the cap. A price moved within one block barely moves it, but the pool may be thin, so holding a moved price for longer may be cheap. What bounds the damage is the fund's total cap (`balanceCap`, fixed at creation), which limits how many dollars can be pending, and the agent's own comparison with Chainlink before converting, which lives off-chain, in the agent, so it doesn't hold against a stolen agent key (see 7). If the oracle is blocked (`observe` reverts with `OLD`, see [Known risks](#known-risks)), `contributeDollar`, `convert` and, while dollars are pending, `contribute` (through the cap check) revert; votes, payments, reimbursements, `GiveBack`, the exits and `payFee` don't use the oracle: a denial of service, not a loss. Dollars sent to the fund directly don't make `contribute` read the pool: only pending dollars do (`:259`). A fund without a dollar never reads a pool (`:238`, `:249`, `:259`). The factory checks that the pool serves the TWAP when the fund is created, but not its depth; the app picks the pools.
6. **Unchecked external calls: PASS.** Every token movement uses `SafeERC20`: `safeTransferFrom` (`:303`) and `safeTransfer` (`:408`, `:434`, `:587-588`, `:653-654`, `:664`). Incoming amounts are measured by balance difference (`:302-304`, `:333-334`, `:347-348`); with a test token that burns 1% of every transfer, contributions in the currency are credited, and dollar contributions kept as pending, only for what arrived. The ignored returns of `pool.swap` and `pool.observe` are covered under Slither, and the factory turns any revert of `observe` into `InvalidPool` (`FundFactoryV2.sol:52-55`).
7. **Centralization: PASS.** `FundV2` and `FundFactoryV2` have no owner, admin, pause or upgrade path. The factory's Uniswap factory and `feeRecipient` are immutable (`FundFactoryV2.sol:20-21`), and so is each fund's `feeBps`, at most 1% (`:132`, `:186`). `feeRecipient` can only receive `feeOwed` through `payFee`; if it were blocklisted, only `payFee` would fail, which is why the fee is paid apart instead of on every contribution. The agent can only `convert` (what arrives is credited to the members whose dollars were converted), `reimburseAgreedExpense` (to a member, within `weeklyCap` per week, `:421-426`) and `propose` (at most 10 a week, `:449`). It never votes: only a member's proposal carries the proposer's vote (`:464`), and `vote` is `onlyMember` (`:467`). It can never be a member (`:201`, `:532`, `:562`). A stolen agent key can cost at most `weeklyCap` a week, paid to members, and `weeklyCap` can't exceed `balanceCap` (`:185`, `:524`). On top of that, each conversion can lose up to 2% against the TWAP. An attacker who also holds the pool's price for 30 minutes (see 5) moves that floor too, and can then convert every member's pending dollars (`:328-330`) in a sandwiched swap, losing the group up to all the dollars pending at that moment, which `balanceCap` limits (it counts them, with the available currency, at `:257-260`). Dollars sent to the fund directly are not pending, so `convert` can't touch them. N always stays below the number of members (`:184`, `:528`, `:577`). The tokens are upgradeable, as in v1 (see 9 and 10).
8. **Events: PASS.** Every state change emits an event: `Contribution` (with `credited = 0` for dollars), one `Conversion` per member, `Reimbursement`, `ProposalCreated`, `VoteCast` and `ProposalExecuted`; each executed action adds its own (`Payment`, `MemberAdded`, `MemberRemoved` with the currency and the dollars returned, `MemberReplaced`, `WeeklyCapChanged`, `VotesRequiredChanged`, `AgentChanged`, `ContributionGivenBack`, `FundClosed`); `distributeRemainder` emits `RemainderDistributed`, `payFee` emits `FeePaid`, and the factory emits `FundCreated`. `totalContributed`, `totalPendingDollar` and `feeOwed` have no event of their own, but every change to them comes with one that carries the amounts (the fee is `amount - credited` in `Contribution` and `currencyOut - credited` in `Conversion`). `FundClosed` and `RemainderDistributed` emit the available currency (without `feeOwed`) and the dollar balance, read before the split. Tests pin the exact data of every v2 event except the four ported unchanged from v1 (`ProposalCreated`, `VoteCast`, `ProposalExecuted` and `VotesRequiredChanged`).
9. **Tokens with a blocklist: FAIL (known risk, governance workaround).** As in v1, payouts are pushed. If a member who is owed currency or dollars (their pending dollars or a part of the split) gets blocklisted by that token, closing (`:653-654`) and removing that member (`:587-588`) revert for everyone, as do payments to them. Workaround: vote `ReplaceMember` to another address of the same member (it moves no tokens and carries their pending dollars), then close. After closing, members can't be replaced any more, so a member blocklisted later blocks `distributeRemainder` for both tokens whenever they are owed anything, until the blocklist is lifted. There is no admin to rescue it, by design. The fee doesn't go through members: `payFee` only pays `feeRecipient`. If the fund itself were blocklisted, its balance of that token would be frozen and, while it holds any, so would the close, and with it the other token.
10. **Non-standard tokens: known risk (accepted).** The accounting assumes both tokens are standard ERC-20s: no rebasing down, no fee charged to the sender on top of the amount, and no issuer taking tokens out of the fund. A fee taken from what the receiver gets is handled, since contributions count only what arrived. Every path in the contract keeps `Σ pendingDollar == totalPendingDollar ≤` the dollar balance and `feeOwed ≤` the currency balance (the invariants test both); only the token could break them. If it did, `_split` would underflow (`:643`) and the fund couldn't close, or `availableCurrency()` would (`:231`) and almost nothing would work. A saturating subtraction wouldn't help: the pending dollars still couldn't all be paid. `convert`'s exact-debit check (`:347`) already rejects a dollar that takes more than `total` from the fund. Ripio's wFIATs and Celo's USDT behave as assumed today, but they are upgradeable.
11. **The fee never reaches a member: PASS.** `feeOwed` is set aside from what arrives (`:278-280`) and from each converted share (`:389`, `:396`), always in the currency. `availableCurrency()` subtracts it (`:231`), and every payment (`:433`), share (`:557`), split (`:639`) and cap check (`:258`) uses it, never the raw balance. Tests check it for reimbursements, `Pay`, `GiveBack`, removal and the close, and the invariants check that `feeOwed` stays backed by the currency balance and that `feeRecipient` only ever gets fees. `payFee` works with the fund open or closed (`:660`). The fee rounds down: a contribution under `BPS / feeBps` wei (400 wei at 25 bps) pays none, which saves far less than the gas it costs. Since `contributed` is net (`:281`, `:393`), `GiveBack` pays and subtracts the same amount, and the fee isn't given back (`:547-549`).
12. **Rounding: PASS.** Conversion shares round down (`:388`), so what is credited plus the fee never exceeds what arrived (`testFuzz_convertSplitNeverExceedsOut`); what is left over belongs to no member and stays in the available currency, which the close splits by `contributed`. Shares on removal and at the close round down too (`:557`, `:650-651`), and the dust stays in the fund for the next `distributeRemainder`. Once only dust is left, `distributeRemainder` may move nothing and still emit `RemainderDistributed` with that dust, and anyone can repeat it at their own gas cost (same in v1), so the agent and the web should treat dust-sized ones as noise.
13. **Denial of service: PASS.** Every loop is bounded by the member list, at most 10: the constructor, `voteCount`, `_indexOf`, `_split`, and `convert`'s lists, since each entry must be a distinct member with dollars pending (`:367-370`). Each member and the agent can make at most 10 proposals a week (`:449`), with notes of at most 560 bytes (`:448`), and can only use up their own count; nothing iterates over proposals. A conversion the pool can only fill in part reverts (`:347`) instead of leaving dollars that no one has pending. Dollars sent directly belong to no one and can't be converted or paid out before the close, so they never count toward `balanceCap` and can't block a contribution, however many are sent; the donor loses everything they sent, which the members split at the close. With nothing pending, `contribute` never reads the pool, so a single wei of dollar can't make it depend on the oracle either. Currency sent directly still counts toward `balanceCap` (it is money the fund can pay), as in v1, and a `Pay` vote brings the balance back under the cap, at a cost to the donor of everything they sent. A fund without a dollar never reads a pool, so dollars sent to it block nothing.
